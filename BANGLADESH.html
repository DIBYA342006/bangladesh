1. The Language Movement (Bhasha Andolon) — 1952

In 1952, a major movement started for protecting the mother language of Bangladeshis (Bangla). Following the 1947 partition of the Indian subcontinent, Pakistan was created in two geographically, culturally, and linguistically distinct wings: West Pakistan and East Pakistan (now Bangladesh).

Despite the majority of the total population residing in East Pakistan and speaking Bengali, the central leadership declared Urdu as the sole national language of Pakistan. Students and intellectuals organized peaceful processions, demanding official recognition for Bengali.

Crucial Milestone: On February 21, 1952, police fired upon student protesters near Dhaka University. Martyrs including Rafiq, Salam, Barkat, and Jabbar sacrificed their lives. In recognition of this struggle, UNESCO declared February 21 as International Mother Language Day, now observed globally each year[cite: 1].

The Language Movement is regarded as the cultural seed that grew into the political demand for complete self-determination and eventual independence.

2. Background & Roots of the Conflict

The struggle was not sudden; it developed over two decades due to systemic inequality between West and East Pakistan:

Political Neglect

East Pakistan had the larger population, yet central government power and high offices were dominated by leaders in West Pakistan.

Economic Disparity

Revenue generated from East Pakistani exports (such as raw jute and tea) was disproportionately spent on Western development projects.

1970 Cyclone Response

When the Bhola Cyclone devastated East Pakistan in November 1970, the sluggish central relief effort alienated the Bengali population.

The 1970 Elections

The Awami League won an outright democratic majority, yet the military regime refused to convene the assembly or transfer authority[cite: 1].

3. The 1971 Liberation War

After a long 9-month war, the people claimed their liberation[cite: 1]. The 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War was a conflict that resulted in the separation of East Pakistan and the creation of the independent nation of Bangladesh[cite: 1].

Core Summary Breakdown:

  • Causes: Decades of political, economic, and cultural discrimination by West Pakistan against the Bengali majority in East Pakistan culminated in the denial of power to Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's Awami League, which won the 1970 elections[cite: 1].
  • Conflict & Atrocities: On March 25, 1971, the Pakistani military launched Operation Searchlight, causing massive casualties and forcing 10 million refugees into India[cite: 1]. Sheikh Mujibur Rahman declared independence on March 26[cite: 1].
  • Resistance: The Bengali guerrilla force, the Mukti Bahini, fought the Pakistani army with training, arms, and sanctuary provided by India[cite: 1]. They operated across rivers, rural paths, and towns to disrupt military supply lines.
  • Indian Intervention: Following preemptive airstrikes by Pakistan on December 3, 1971, India entered the war directly, forming a joint command with the Mukti Bahini[cite: 1].
  • Outcome: Facing defeat, the Pakistani military surrendered unconditionally on December 16, 1971, when Lieutenant General A. A. K. Niazi signed the Instrument of Surrender in Dhaka, yielding 93,000 troops[cite: 1].

The Mukti Bahini (Liberation Army) was composed of regular military soldiers, police personnel, students, farmers, and everyday citizens. They used asymmetric guerrilla tactics across the delta's rivers, disrupting transport convoys and protecting civilian areas until the formal joint advance in December[cite: 1].
The Central Shaheed Minar in Dhaka is the national monument erected to honor the martyrs of the 1952 Language Movement. Every year on February 21 at dawn, people walk barefoot to place flowers at its base during the *Prabhat Pheri* memorial procession.

5. National Symbols & Significance

The National Flag

A green field representing the lush landscape of the country, with a red circle representing the rising sun and the blood sacrificed for freedom.

National Emblem

Features the Shapla (Water Lily) resting on water, flanked on both sides by paddy sheaves and crowned by three tea leaves and four stars.

National Anthem

"Amar Shonar Bangla" (My Golden Bengal), written by Rabindranath Tagore in 1905, was officially adopted as the anthem in 1971.
